# Farm Hand: Florida drought facts for the pitch (read 2026-09-22)

All in our own words, with the source for each. Re-check the water-shortage status the week of ShellHacks.

## How bad the drought is
- Worst, most widespread Florida drought since 2012. (NASA Earth Observatory)
- By April 2026, nearly all of Florida was in at least moderate drought and close to 80% was in extreme drought. (NASA; Agroinformacion)
- Many areas got less than half their normal rain since Sept 1, 2025. St. Petersburg got 7.7 inches vs a normal 19.0, its driest on record for that stretch. (NASA)
- Southwest Florida was 11.4 inches behind on rain as of May 2026. (SWFWMD, June 23 2026)
- The Floridan Aquifer (drinking water + farm irrigation) hit its lowest level since 2011. NASA's GRACE-FO satellites showed north and central Florida aquifers especially dry on March 30, 2026. (NASA; Agroinformacion)
- North Central Florida would need over 1 foot of rain in a single month to meaningfully refill groundwater. (Agroinformacion)

## Water restrictions (the "state famous for rain" hook)
- Southwest Florida Water Management District: Modified Phase III "Extreme" water shortage, declared March 24 2026, extended on June 23 2026 through Oct 1 2026. (SWFWMD)
- Covers 11 full counties (Citrus, DeSoto, Hardee, Hernando, Hillsborough, Manatee, Pasco, Pinellas, Polk, Sarasota, Sumter) plus parts of Charlotte, Highlands, Lake and Marion. (SWFWMD)
- Lawn watering 1 day a week by address, only 12:01–4am or 8pm–11:59pm. (SWFWMD)
- District's reasons: below-average rain, river and lake levels "severely abnormal," public supply "extremely low." (SWFWMD)
- South Florida (Miami, where ShellHacks is): 2-days-a-week year-round schedule plus a 2026 drought advisory. (search summary; verify on SFWMD site)

## Farms
- February 2026 freezes: $3 billion+ in Florida ag losses, then the surviving crops hit weeks of dry heat. (NASA; Agroinformacion)
- Crops hit: watermelons, peanuts, corn, citrus. Suwannee Valley grows about a third of U.S. watermelons on ~10,000 acres. (Agroinformacion)
- Farmers had to irrigate just to prep soil for planting, a step that normally relies on rain. (Agroinformacion)

## Fire
- 1,500+ wildfires in the first 3 months of 2026, on pace to pass the 3,100 in all of 2025. (Agroinformacion)

## Water waste (why our project matters)
- Farm irrigation = 40% of Florida's freshwater withdrawals. (USGS, 2010 data)
- Up to 50% of irrigation water is wasted to evaporation, wind and runoff from inefficient watering. (UF/IFAS-linked guidance, My Florida Home Energy)
- A typical homeowner uses up to 50% of household water on irrigation. (same)

## Real farmers, real quotes (pulled 2026-09-22)
- Andy Seiler, peanut farmer, Seiler Farms, Ocala: "We've had to irrigate some of our fields just to prepare to plant, which is not typical." (WUFT, 2026-03-11)
- Grayson Clark, CEO of Flores Family Plant Nursery, Levy County: their wells pull from the aquifer, and with no rain "we have to drill deeper and deeper." (WUFT)
- North Florida was 3-6 inches behind on rain early in 2026. Marion County got under 10 inches all winter. (WUFT)
- Loran Brookins, Chiefland farmer: uses drip irrigation + SOIL MOISTURE PROBES to decide when to water. Real farmers already do what we automate. (WCJB, 2026-03-27)
- Bob Hochmuth (UF/IFAS): overhead sprinklers use way more water than drip, so drought hurts those farms most. (WCJB)
- David Tuthill, River & Root Farm, Gainesville (blueberries): 70% crop loss, even while watering daily in March-April. (Alligator, June 2026)
- April 2026 was the driest April in 132 years of records. 61 Florida counties declared drought disaster areas. (Alligator; USDA FSA 2026-04-20)
- St. Johns River Water Management District declared a Phase 3 Extreme Water Shortage on May 13, 2026. (Alligator)
- Feb 2026 freezes (winter storms Ezra and Gianna): $3.1B in crop damage. Strawberries lost ~80%, blueberries ~90%. (FreshPlaza)
- STILL GOING: on Sept 16 2026, USDA declared Escambia, Orange, Osceola and Seminole counties drought disaster areas.
  Orange/Osceola/Seminole = Orlando area. About 43% of Florida was in drought as of Sept 15 2026. (USDA FSA; search summary)
