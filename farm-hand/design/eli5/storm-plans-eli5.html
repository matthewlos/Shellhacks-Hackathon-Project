How scattered storm plans could become one table contractors trust

Step 1

Every big Florida utility files a 10-year storm plan.

FPL, Duke, TECO and FPUC each file one with the Florida PSC. It lists what they'll harden, where and when.

Step 2

Each plan is a long PDF in its own format.

The useful part, like miles of tree trimming by county and year, is buried in tables across hundreds of pages.

Step 3

Contractors dig through them by hand to find work to bid on.

Tree trimming around lines is called vegetation management. Miss it in the PDF and you miss the bid.

Step 4

An AI agent team reads every PDF and pulls out each job.

Each row: utility, type of work, county, year, how much. Same agents Farm Hand uses to read weather and drought data.

Step 5

A checker throws out rows that don't add up.

Missing year, wrong county, totals that don't match: flagged, not passed on. Sperry's job post asks for exactly this.

Step 6

Contractors bid from one clean table.

Every utility's planned work in one place, and every row checked before anyone sees it.
